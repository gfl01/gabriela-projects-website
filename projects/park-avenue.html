Buying a prewar apartment in Manhattan often means falling in love with the building before the interiors. The beamed ceilings, generous windows and classic proportions are all there, but the finishes have usually been updated one decade at a time, and the layout rarely fits the way a family lives today. Our client had just closed on her apartment on Park Avenue and felt exactly that way. She had a clear vision for her new home but needed a team to guide her through the design, the approvals and the construction.

From our first meeting, we knew the layout had to come first. Together with one of our architects, we redesigned the floor plan around her family's daily routine. We also prepared the drawings and managed the approval process, which is one of the most demanding steps of any renovation in a Manhattan building.

Because our client knew so well what she wanted, the interior design became a true collaboration. We worked side by side on every decision, from the stone slabs to the cabinet hardware. Only once the design was complete and every approval was in hand did our construction team begin.

01

The kitchen

The kitchen was the heart of the transformation. The original space was simple and dated, with flat white cabinets, a cramped corner sink and a black dishwasher that interrupted the counter line. We replaced it with custom cream cabinetry and fully integrated panel appliances so the room reads as furniture rather than a utility space.

The star of the kitchen is the stone. We chose a dramatic violet-veined marble and carried it from the waterfall island straight up the backsplash in one continuous slab. Paired with a black range, brass hardware and slim black pendants, the kitchen feels bold without losing its warmth.

We also turned the window corner into a cozy breakfast nook. A built-in banquette and a small round table make it the perfect spot for slow coffee mornings and for the kids to sit down for breakfast or an afternoon snack, framed by classic wall paneling that echoes the character of the building.

02

Arched doors and white oak floors

To connect the kitchen with the living and dining rooms, we designed a pair of arched glass doors framed in dark wood. They let light and views flow through the apartment while still allowing the kitchen to be closed off when needed. They quickly became the home's signature detail.

Throughout the home, we installed Haddonfield white oak floors from PID Floors. We used a herringbone pattern in the main social areas and straight planks everywhere else. At the entry, black and cream marble tiles set on the diagonal give guests a striking first impression.

03

Four bathrooms

We renovated four of the apartment's five bathrooms: the primary bathroom, two secondary bathrooms and the guest powder room. Each one was designed with its own personality, while sharing the same attention to detail and quality of materials that runs through the rest of the home.

In the primary bathroom, we kept the shower layout and completely refreshed the space around it, adding a double vanity so the room works comfortably for two.

In one of the secondary bathrooms, the transformation speaks for itself. Gone are the bathtub, the standard white tile with cobalt blue trim and the glass shelves. In their place, marble hexagon mosaic now wraps the room from floor to ceiling, and a frameless glass walk-in shower makes the room feel larger and easier to use every day. A fluted walnut vanity, brushed brass fixtures and a sculptural beaded mirror bring warmth and personality to the space.

04

From a second kitchen to a girls' bedroom

One of the most meaningful changes happened in a room that used to be a second kitchen. The apartment had been combined from two units, which left our client with a kitchen she simply didn't need. We fully demolished it and turned it into a bedroom for her daughters.

Each girl now has her own arched sleeping alcove with a built-in bed, storage drawers underneath and shelves for her favorite things. Soft floral wallpaper lines the alcoves, and a long desk under the window gives them a shared place to study. It's a room designed to grow with them.

05

A study that doubles as a guest room

The study doubles as a guest bedroom. A wall of dark custom cabinetry with patterned inset panels, matched to the wallpaper, conceals a Murphy bed that folds down when guests arrive. By day, the room is a tailored, cozy space for working and sewing; by night, it becomes a comfortable guest suite with its own private bathroom.

The result

After four months of construction and many coordinations between our trades, suppliers and the building, the apartment was complete. What was once a dated space now feels timeless and personal, a home that honors its prewar roots while truly reflecting the family who lives there. Seeing our client walk in and fall in love with every room is exactly why we do what we do at Gabriela Projects.
