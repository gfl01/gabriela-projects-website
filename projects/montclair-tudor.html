Tudor homes have a charm that's hard to resist. There are steep rooflines, a storybook exterior and a sense of history in every corner. Inside, though, they were built for a different way of living, with small rooms closed off from one another and a layout that rarely fits how a family moves through a home today. Our client loved her Montclair Tudor, but the floor plan simply wasn't working. She dreamed of an open, connected home and needed a team to help her get there.

When we took on the project, it was clear the layout had to come first. The rooms were poorly distributed, so we reworked the floor plan from the ground up as part of a full gut renovation. Our client came to us with plenty of her own ideas, from the feel of the kitchen to the mood of each room. We brought her ideas together with our own design, working through every choice side by side until the whole house felt like her.

The most challenging part of the project was making that open layout possible. The structural columns that divided the main floor had to go. Working alongside a structural engineer, we modified the structure and replaced the columns with steel beams, which opened up the space our client had always imagined. It's the kind of change you don't see in the finished home, but you feel it the moment you walk in.

01

The kitchen

The kitchen is the heart of the new layout. The original room was narrow and traditional, with white cabinets, dark counters, a freestanding work table in the middle and dark wood floors. Gone is the cramped galley feel. In its place is a long, open kitchen that finally connects with the rest of the house.

The star of the kitchen is the island. Wrapped in a marble with bold dark veining that waterfalls down both ends, it offers seating for the whole family and plenty of room to cook, do homework or gather with friends. A wall of full-height black cabinetry keeps storage out of sight, and a stepped ceiling with recessed lighting and slim pendants draws the eye down the length of the room.

02

The living room

In the living room, we kept what gave the house its soul. The original brick fireplace and wood mantel stayed, but we painted the chimney wall a deep charcoal so it now stands out as the focal point of the room. Black-framed windows on either side bring in the garden views, and a brass chandelier adds warmth overhead. The dark stained floors and green walls were replaced with a bright, calm palette that lets the architecture speak.

Throughout the main floor, we installed light white oak floors in a herringbone pattern. They carry from room to room without interruption, tying the new open spaces together and bringing a lighter, more modern feel to a classic home.

03

The entry

The entry tells the story of the whole renovation. The dark wood paneling and heavy, dark-painted ceiling once made the space feel closed in. Today, white walls and a clean beamed ceiling fill it with light, while the dark wood and glass doors keep a nod to the home's Tudor character.

04

The powder room

The powder room was designed to surprise. Gone are the dark wood cabinet, the framed mirror and the gray tile floor. In their place, textured charcoal walls wrap the room, and a floating vanity in the same dramatic veined marble as the kitchen holds a vessel sink with a wall-mounted brass faucet. A sculptural mirror, warm sconces and soft lighting under the vanity make it a small room guests remember.

05

The primary bathroom

In the primary bathroom, the transformation speaks for itself. The drop-in tub, pedestal sink and small corner shower felt dated and cramped. We redesigned the room around a freestanding soaking tub, a large glass walk-in shower lined in gray stone and marble herringbone floors. Lit built-in shelves give our client a place for her favorite things, and brushed brass fixtures and a slim pendant bring a touch of warmth. It now feels like a quiet retreat at the end of the day.

06

Upstairs

Upstairs, one of the secondary bedrooms became a calm, bright retreat. Soft neutral walls, recessed lighting and plush carpet underfoot make it a comfortable, easy room to settle into.

07

The exterior

The renovation reached the outside of the house too. We replaced the windows with new black-framed ones, keeping part of the originals. The dark frames echo the Tudor half-timbering and give the white facade a crisp, updated look.

The result

After six months of construction and close coordination between our structural engineer, trades and suppliers, the house was complete. What was once a beautiful but divided Tudor now flows the way our client always imagined. It's open, bright and full of life, and it still honors the character that made her fall in love with it. Helping a client turn a long-held dream into the home she lives in every day is exactly why we do what we do at Gabriela Projects.
