High-rise apartments on the Upper East Side come with something money can't easily buy: wide windows and sweeping views of the city. Many of them, though, still carry the finishes of an earlier era, with mirrored walls, glass block and polished stone that make a home feel cold and dated. Our clients, a young family, loved their apartment's light and views, but the interiors didn't feel like theirs. They wanted a warm, modern home that reflected who they are.

The clients already had an interior designer with a clear vision, and we joined the team to bring it to life. We worked hand in hand with the designer, from choosing materials to solving every detail on site, so that what was imagined on paper became real in the home. Our focus was the social areas, the floors throughout the apartment, the custom millwork and a new layout that gave the family more flexibility.

01

Living, dining and kitchen

The open living, dining and kitchen area was where the change was most needed. The mirrored wall, the curved glass-block counter and the track lighting made the space feel like it belonged to someone else. Today, the room is calm and inviting. In the kitchen, we kept the existing cabinetry and focused on what made the biggest difference. The glass block is gone, new white stone tops the counters and island, and brass pendants add a warm touch above. A smooth ceiling with recessed lights lets the city views take center stage.

We replaced the floors throughout the apartment, trading the cold, glossy marble for warm oak. In the living and dining area, the oak is laid in a herringbone pattern, while a soft checkered tile marks the kitchen, defining each zone without closing anything off.

02

The media wall

The mirrored wall in the living room became the heart of the room: a custom media wall. The TV sits flush in a clean white surface above a fluted wood base and a gray stone ledge, and open wood shelving tucked beside the window holds books and family keepsakes. It's where the family gathers at the end of the day, with the skyline right behind them.

03

A nursery and a flexible room

One of the bedrooms became a nursery full of charm. The pale blue walls and laminate floor gave way to wide oak planks and a whimsical safari wallpaper above classic panel molding. Under the window, we built a custom bench that hides the heating and air conditioning units and adds storage for toys and blankets, making smart use of every inch. It's a soft, happy room for the newest member of this young, lovely family, and one that can easily grow with them.

We also rethought the layout to give the family a flexible room. It works as a home office during the week and turns into a guest bedroom whenever family comes to visit.

04

The hallway

The hallway shows how far the apartment has come. Gone are the mirrored closet doors, the speckled wall finish and the dark patterned door at the end of the corridor. In their place, graceful arches and panel molding frame the path through the home, and the herringbone oak floor carries all the way through, filling the hallway with light and warmth.

The result

After about two months of construction and close coordination with the interior designer, our trades and the building, the apartment was complete. What once felt like a time capsule now feels fresh, warm and personal. Seeing a family truly feel at home in their space is why we do what we do at Gabriela Projects.
